The South African Story

Reconciliation in South Africa is not a historical event that was resolved at a particular moment. It is an ongoing demand that the present makes on everyone who lives here — regardless of when they arrived or what they inherited. The divisions that run through communities now are not echoes. They are structural, immediate, and felt.

One33 exists inside that reality. Not as an outside interpreter, and not as a prescriber of solutions. As a presence that asks, listens, and then connects the people and resources that can do the restorative work.

The organisation works across three sectors — public, private, and social — identifying partners who are context-specific rather than generic. Some will recur. Others will be brought in for a particular need, then step back. This is not inefficiency. It is fidelity to what communities actually require.

"The need is not to be named. It is to be heard."

Twenty-plus years of restorative justice work in South Africa has made one thing clear: the communities that benefit most are those that were asked first, not told. One33 is built around that single conviction.
