# One33 — Hero Video Concept

**Status:** Proposed. Josh to build a basic version for Richard to react to before sourcing begins in earnest.

---

## Concept

A short montage of South African landscape and cultural footage, edited together without text overlay. The sequence gives visitors something to feel before they're asked to think — grounding the site in place and setting up the confrontation that the copy delivers as the page unfolds.

No narration. No captions. No poverty imagery. People are shown as agents, not subjects.

The edit should feel open and unhurried — cuts that breathe, not cuts that rush. Somewhere between 30 and 60 seconds, looping cleanly.

---

## Shots

### 1. Aerial — City and township in the same frame
The confrontation shot. Johannesburg or Cape Town from above, formal city and informal settlement visible together. Not exploitative — the camera doesn't linger — but honest about the geography of inequality. This is the visual problem statement.

**Search terms:** `Johannesburg aerial drone`, `Soweto aerial footage`, `South Africa urban divide aerial`

---

### 2. Wide landscape — Drakensberg or Karoo
Vast, still, elemental. Places the story in South Africa and gives the sequence room to breathe. A counterweight to the density of the city shot.

**Search terms:** `Drakensberg landscape drone`, `Karoo wide landscape`, `South Africa landscape cinematic`

---

### 3. Busy taxi rank or transport hub
Movement, density, people sharing space without connection. Sets up the problem quietly — proximity without relationship. Park Station, a Johannesburg or Durban taxi rank.

**Search terms:** `South Africa taxi rank`, `Park Station Johannesburg`, `Johannesburg commuters footage`

---

### 4. Street-level community scene
Vendors, colour, life. A township or city street that reads as alive rather than impoverished. Human-scale, people as agents.

**Search terms:** `South Africa street life`, `Cape Town Bo-Kaap street`, `Durban street market footage`

---

### 5. Two people in close conversation
Unposed if possible. Different backgrounds. Close proximity. This is the mission — what One33 is building toward. If genuinely unposed dialogue footage proves hard to find without looking staged, a close shot of hands — different skin tones, working or resting together — often reads more honestly.

**Search terms:** `South Africa people conversation`, `community dialogue South Africa`, `South Africa human connection footage`

---

## Where to Source

- **Artgrid** — best quality, paid
- **Pexels** — free, worth checking first
- **Envato Elements** — broad library, subscription-based
- **YouTube** (CC licence) — useful for aerial and landscape shots; search with `"free to use"` or `"creative commons"` appended

---

## Notes

- Avoid anything that reads as documentary poverty footage or township tourism
- Test: does each shot show people as agents rather than subjects?
- The aerial shot (shot 1) is the hardest to get right tonally — worth reviewing a few options before committing
- Final edit decision (cut timing, music if any) to be made once footage is in hand
