# One33 — Discovery Session Notes

## What is One33?

A South African reconciliation organisation. Exists to build the nation towards reconciliation — the kind that requires taking responsibility for what's been done wrong and actively restoring what's been lost.

Two primary functions:

1. **Relational** — facilitating reconciliatory dialogue processes within and between communities (broken by race, gender, culture, generation, etc.)
2. **Practical** — serving as a connector between organisations, government, skill sets, and communities to facilitate actual restoration

One33 does the relational work directly. It facilitates the practical work — it doesn't do the restoration itself.

Key distinction from most nonprofits: works bottom-up. Asks communities what they've lost rather than prescribing solutions. Reverse-engineering the need, not enforcing help.

---

## Who They Work With

Three sectors:

- Public (government)
- Private (business)
- Social/nonprofit

Partners are context-specific. Some will be recurring, others brought in for a particular need. Part of One33's role is identifying and connecting the right partners for each situation.

## Website Goals

- Give enough information that people quickly understand what One33 is and why it's distinct
- Capture hearts, not just inform minds
- Enable storytelling over time (video, image + text)
- Simple, spacious — one thought at a time as you scroll
- Emotionally resonant without being manipulative
- Honest and direct — justice language, not soft or ambiguous

Success looks like someone coming off the site saying:

- "I get the heartbeat of this organisation"
- "I never thought of reconciliation that way before"
- "I understand why this isn't just another nonprofit"
- "I know how I could be part of this"

---

## Target Audience

Not the masses. People who are likely to engage in some form of partnership — financial giving, skills, relationships, or organisational connections. Could come from anywhere but the site speaks to those with something to contribute.

## Brand & Feel

- Distinct from Anthem Church
- Logo by Sade — black and white. Name renders as **One33** or **ONE33** (not the numeral "133"). Richard is not precious about the logo but likes the written-out form with numerals and the Ubuntu-style feel.
- Three brand words (working): **Resolved. Grounded. Human.** — intended as an active design and copy filter, not decorative language. *(Later replaced in Session 3 by Richard's words: Emotional. Aggrieved. Grateful.)*
- Image-driven over typography-driven
- South African story — not trying to be generic or global
- No poverty imagery
- Authentic human imagery — faces, relationships, restoration in action
- AI-generated or stock images acceptable as long as they feel real

**Feeling on site:** confronted (not aggressively) by the reality that people around you are not okay — and drawn into a clear, purposeful avenue to help. Strong, just, ordered. But human and spacious. Room to breathe between thoughts.

## Social Media

Deliberately holding off. No strategy until there are real stories to tell. Preference for word-of-mouth over manufactured reach. Cautious about inadvertently telling Domino Foundation stories, since that's where the 20+ years of restorative justice work was done.
